Colour

Reason: true black saves OLED battery and keeps night vision. Each accent has exactly one job so colour can be read before words. Red is never used for anything but SOS and real danger.

Type

Reason: condensed numerals stay big in narrow space and read at arm's length; two weights keep hierarchy from becoming noise. Sheet uses fallback fonts; Barlow, Barlow Condensed and Noto Sans Devanagari are bundled in M0.

StatusTag

Reason: the first thing a tired trekker needs is "am I OK?". Icon shape + word + colour: the shape and word still work for colour-blind users and in sunlight.

  • Shapes differ per state: circle-check, fork, triangle, u-turn, crosshair-slash, rings, octagon.
  • Red only on SOS.
  • No motion on the tag itself; the HUD tint and tag change together in 220 ms.

Glance zone: DirectionDial, HeroInstruction, StatTriplet

Reason: one dominant answer ("what do I do next?") with the three numbers a trekker actually asks for: how far, how much climb, how long. The whole HUD changes together so one glance is enough.

Countdown

Reason: after a detected fall the user may be dazed. One huge number and a draining ring say how long until SOS goes out. The ring is a live loop, so it is allowed to move; everything else stays still.

BigInstruction and StatusChecklist

Reason: in an emergency the user needs one sentence to follow and the truth about what has actually happened. Big text never claims more than is true: nothing delivered = "SOS active", partly = "delivered to 2 of 3", all = "SOS delivered". Rows list what the app has done, in plain words. "Queued" is never shown as "delivered".

  • Status words replace icons-only states: queued, sending, delivered, broadcasting, armed, off.
  • Only live things pulse (beacon); done rows are static.
  • Row labels are sentences a scared person can read aloud to a rescuer.

LargeToggle

Reason: torch and siren must be switchable with a gloved thumb, and the user must see at once whether they are on. State is shown by fill + word + icon, never colour alone. 88 dp tall (110 dp in glove mode).

  • Off: outline, word "Off". On: solid orange fill, black text, word "On".
  • Haptic 1 short on every change. Siren plays on the alarm stream, so silent mode does not mute it.
  • Single tap is allowed: both are reversible and help the user; neither sends anything to other people.
  • A11y role button, pressed state announced ("Siren, on").

SnoozeButton

Reason: when someone leaves the trail on purpose, the repeating off-route buzz becomes noise and they stop trusting alerts. One big button in the thumb zone silences that alert for 10 minutes, shows the time left, and a tap undoes it. Only off-route alerts are silenced: SOS, friend and danger alerts never are.

  • Appears only in Off route state, above the dock.
  • Height 72 dp (90 dp glove).
  • Haptic 10 ms tick on snooze; 1 short when alerts resume. The off-route 3-long pattern stops during the snooze.
  • Counts down live (real 10 min in this sheet); pauses nothing else.

ThumbBack

Reason: the system back gesture is hard with gloves, a stick in the other hand, or a wet screen. A back control at the bottom edge, on the thumb side, is always reachable. Toggle "Left-handed" at the top to mirror it.

Toast (received quick message)

Reason: a friend's "Wait" or "Take left fork" must be seen without opening anything. One line at the bottom of the glance zone with sender and message; the voice reads it aloud after a chime. It adds height to the glance zone (pushing the map down) so it never covers the dial, the hero or the numbers. Auto-hides after 6 s.

  • One line, sender in cyan, message in primary text, ellipsis if long.
  • Auto-hide 6 s, 220 ms fade. A new message replaces the current one.
  • Voice chime then "Rahul says: Wait" (English or Hindi), ducking music.
  • Not a button: nothing to tap, so nothing can be hit by accident; full history lives in Timeline.
  • A11y polite live region.

PinTypeGrid

Reason: marking a place must take one tap while walking. Six large tiles, 2×3, each ≥ 88 dp tall. Position and GPS time are saved on tap; the note comes afterwards.

 
  • Danger uses the warn colour on the icon only (red is reserved).
  • Haptic 1 short on save.
  • Tile labels are words; the icon is secondary.

ReadinessRow

Reason: before a trek the only question is "am I ready?". Each requirement is a row with honest state (including age) and one-tap fix when something is missing.

  • State shown by icon shape (check / triangle / octagon) and words.
  • Fix button ≥ 56 dp wide in thumb reach; disappears when resolved.
  • Weather row shows saved time: "saved 5 h ago".

HealthBanner

Reason: slow-building risks (altitude, storms) need to be noticed without interrupting the trail. One line in the warn colour, tap for detail, dismissible with a 56 dp target.

  • Priority Health is below GroupGap; banner never blocks the dock.
  • Dismiss hides until condition worsens or 2 h pass.

ElevationProfile

Reason: the profile is the honest summary of effort ahead: where the climbs are, how high camp is. It is real route data, not decoration. "You are here" turns it into a progress bar.

  • Data in this sheet is a sample 12.4 km route.
  • Passed section is bright; ahead is muted.
  • Axis labels in km and m, 12 px minimum; label of current altitude is the one prominent number.

Map layers

Reason: the map is only context. Contours stay nearly invisible (muted-line) so the route (cyan, 4 px), your breadcrumb (faint dots), friends (rings with initials) and you (orange dot with heading cone) read first. Unchosen branches are grey and turn warn when taken.

  • Altitude caption bottom-right: 3,214 m.
  • Friend rings: solid ok = in range; dashed text-3 = out of range, hollow, with age.
  • GPS lost: hollow position with growing uncertainty circle (not a stale solid dot).
  • Sunlight mode strengthens contours and doubles line widths.
